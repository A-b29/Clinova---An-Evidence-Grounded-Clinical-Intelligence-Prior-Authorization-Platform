# Illustrative Sample Policy: Total Knee Arthroplasty (TKA)

Applies to procedure codes: 27447

> ILLUSTRATIVE SAMPLE TEXT FOR AN EDUCATIONAL PROTOTYPE. This is not a real
> payer policy, not medical guidance, and must not be used for coverage decisions.

## Scope

This sample policy describes example documentation that a reviewer may look for
when a prior-authorization request is submitted for total knee arthroplasty
(knee replacement surgery). It applies to primary total knee arthroplasty only.

## Criterion 1: Diagnosis

The clinical documentation should state the diagnosis that supports the knee
replacement request, such as osteoarthritis or degenerative joint disease of
the knee, rheumatoid arthritis, or avascular necrosis of the knee. The diagnosis
on the request should be consistent with the diagnosis in the clinical note.

## Criterion 2: Imaging findings

The documentation should include imaging of the affected knee, for example
weight-bearing radiographs (X-rays), describing the findings. Example findings
include advanced joint space narrowing, bone-on-bone contact, osteophytes, or
subchondral sclerosis. The imaging description should be consistent across the
submitted documents.

## Criterion 3: Conservative treatment

The documentation should describe nonsurgical (conservative) management of
knee symptoms and the patient's response. Examples include supervised physical
therapy, anti-inflammatory medication (NSAIDs), corticosteroid or hyaluronic
acid injections, weight loss, bracing, or activity modification, with a
statement that relief was inadequate.

## Criterion 4: Persistent symptoms and clinical rationale

The documentation should describe persistent knee symptoms, such as pain,
stiffness, or instability, including their duration, and explain how they limit
function or daily activities. The note should give the clinical rationale for
proceeding to knee replacement.

## Documentation reminders

Reviewers should confirm that the requested procedure, laterality, and
diagnosis are consistent across the request and the note. Missing information
should be requested from the provider rather than assumed.
